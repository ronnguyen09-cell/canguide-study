Purpose of Study

You are invited to take part in a research study because you are a second-year family medicine resident (PGY2, which means postgraduate year 2) or an attending family doctor in the Department of Family Medicine at Queen’s University. This form explains the study. It will help you decide if you want to take part. This study has received ethical approval from the Queen’s University Health Sciences and Affiliated Teaching Hospitals Research Ethics Board (HSREB).

Taking part is your choice. You can choose not to take part in any part of the study, at any time. In this study, you will try a web app for six weeks and then fill out a one-time survey. If you say no, nothing changes for you. It will not affect your evaluations, your standing in the program or your job.

Why we are doing this study. CanGuide Med is a web app. It holds short summaries of Canadian clinical practice guidelines for office family practice. It also has built-in calculators for scores used at the point of care. Ron Nguyen built the app as his resident research project. We want to learn what family doctors think of it. We want to know if it is useful, if it is easy to use, and if you trust what it says.

How the app was made. Each summary was written from the full text of its main source. An artificial intelligence (AI) writing tool helped with the drafts. Each summary was then checked against its source, point by point, with Ron Nguyen reviewing. The app does not use AI when you open it. You see fixed text.

The trial. From December 1, 2026 to January 12, 2027, you can use the app as much or as little as you like. The link is https://study.canguide.ca. You do not need an account. You do not have to use the app at all.

The survey. After the trial, we will email you a link to one online survey. The survey is on Qualtrics, a survey website that Queen’s University uses. It takes about 10 minutes. It asks how useful the app was, how easy it was to use, and how much you trust it. It also asks how often you used the app and if you are a resident or an attending doctor. There is space for comments. The survey will stay open until about February 2, 2027.

Risks and benefits

The risks of this study are small. A summary in the app could have a mistake or be out of date. The app does not replace the full guideline or your own judgment. Please check the source before you act, above all for drug doses. If you see a mistake, please email Ron Nguyen. We will fix mistakes that we confirm. We will email everyone invited about any serious mistake.

The survey does not ask for your name. There is still a small chance that someone could guess who you are from your answers. To lower this chance, we will only report results for groups.

You may or may not benefit from taking part in this study. You may find the app handy for quick look-ups.

We hope that the results of this study may benefit family doctors and residents who use guideline tools in the future.

Confidentiality

The app. The app has no accounts. It does not ask you to type in any information about yourself or anyone else. Nothing is sent anywhere. The app collects nothing. The only storage is on your own device, in your browser, for display. We cannot see it. If you use the search box or a calculator, what you enter stays in your browser. Please do not enter patient names.

The survey. This survey is anonymous, which means we will not collect any personal identifying information, such as your name or email address. The survey is set so that it does not record your Internet Protocol (IP) address or your location. You can skip any question. Because this survey is anonymous, you can stop at any time by closing your browser. Once you submit the survey, you cannot withdraw your answers, since there is no way to identify them.

Your answers will go straight into Qualtrics. We will keep the data on password-protected Queen’s University systems for at least five years after the study ends. After this time, we will destroy the data.

We will share the results as group totals only. We plan to present them at the Department of Family Medicine Primary Care Research Day on February 25, 2027. We may also publish them or present them at meetings. No one will be named.

The person(s) in charge of the data during storage is/are Ron Nguyen, the Principal Investigator, and Dr. Mark Braidwood, the faculty supervisor. Some groups and their staff may see or get copies of some study information to check the data and make sure the study is safe and being done correctly. These may include:

  • Study team members approved by the Principal Investigator.
  • Authorized staff from Queen’s University, its hospitals, and Queen’s Research Ethics Board (GREB).

When the Principal Investigator completes residency, the faculty supervisor will become the custodian of the data, which will stay on Queen’s-managed systems.

Conflict of interest

Ron Nguyen has a conflict of interest to declare related to this study. Details are as follows: he built CanGuide Med, and he is also the person studying it. He is a resident in the same department as the people invited. Some attending doctors teach or grade residents. To lower any pressure to take part, the invitation goes to the whole group by email, the survey is anonymous, and we cannot tell who took part.

This study has no funding. No company is involved. The app is free and is not being sold.
